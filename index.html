    If I take nicotine again now

      Craving right now? Wait 5 minutes

      Most urges peak and fade within 3 to 5 minutes. Start the timer and use a tip below.

      5:00Ready when you are

      Ways to ride it out

      • Delay: tell yourself "not for 10 more minutes", then repeat.
      • Breathe: in for 4 seconds, out for 6, five times.
      • Drink: a glass of cold water, slowly.
      • Move: a 5 minute walk, stairs, or stretching changes your state fast.
      • Change place: leave the spot where you usually use nicotine.
      • Tell someone: text a friend "having a craving, talk to me for 5 min".

      Something for your hands and mouth

      • Sugar-free gum, mints or cinnamon sticks
      • Crunchy snacks: carrots, apple slices, nuts, sunflower seeds
      • A straw or toothpick to chew on
      • A stress ball, pen or fidget toy

      Beat your triggers

      • Coffee or alcohol: cut back for the first weeks, they are strong triggers.
      • After meals: brush your teeth or go for a short walk right away.
      • Stress: try a breathing exercise, music or a shower instead.
      • Boredom: keep a short list of quick things to do (game, call, chores).
      • Sleep and food: tiredness and hunger make cravings stronger, so eat regularly and sleep enough.

      Replacement options

      • Nicotine patch, gum or lozenge: gives a steady, lower dose without smoke or vape chemicals, and you cut down step by step. Useful if withdrawal is hard.
      • Prescription medicines: a doctor can discuss options such as varenicline or bupropion.
      • Quitline or app: free coaching is available in many countries. Search for your local quitline.

      Ask a pharmacist or doctor before starting any replacement, especially if you are pregnant, have heart problems or take other medicines.

      Slow tapering guide

      There is no single "correct" amount, because products and people differ. The common approach is to step down gradually, then stop once you reach a very low level.

      General rule of thumb

      • Cut about 25% every 1 to 2 weeks (some people use 10% for a gentler pace, others 50%).
      • Hold a step longer if cravings feel too strong. Going back a step is fine.
      • Most plans take 4 to 12 weeks. Go slower rather than faster.
      • Cut the easiest uses first (boredom, habit), keep the hardest ones (morning, after meals) for last.

      Examples by product

      • Cigarettes: 20 a day, then 15, 10, 5, then stop. About 1 to 2 mg of nicotine reaches your body per cigarette.
      • Vape: lower the liquid strength step by step, for example 50, 35, 20, 10, 5, then 0 mg/ml, or reduce the number of puffs or pods per day.
      • Pouches or gum: move to a lower strength, then space out the time between uses.
